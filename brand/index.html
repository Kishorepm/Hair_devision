01 · Logo

One mark, four shapes.

The logo has been redrawn as clean vector artwork from the original, so it stays sharp at any size, from an appointment card to a two-metre fascia. Pick the shape that fits the space; never redraw or retype it.

Colourways
Clear space

Keep a margin of x clear on every side, where x is the height of the letter H in “HAIR”, roughly one-tenth of the roundel’s width. No text, edges or other logos inside it.

02 · Colour

Eucalyptus, ink and porcelain.

Three colours do almost all the work. Eucalyptus is sampled directly from the logo. Tap any swatch to copy its HEX value.

How much of each

Mostly porcelain and air, grounded by ink, with eucalyptus as the accent: a ring, a line, one word in italics. If a layout looks green, there is too much of it.

Readable pairings

The number is the contrast ratio. 4.5 or more is comfortable for body text; 3 or more is fine for headlines and logos. Eucalyptus on a light background is for shapes and large type only. Use Deep eucalyptus for small text.

For the printer and signwriter

CMYK values here are calculated starting points, not press-proofed. Ask for a printed proof and approve it against this page on a good screen. For vinyl and paint, have the supplier match their nearest stock colour to a printed swatch rather than to a code, and keep the sample they choose, so every future job matches it.

04 · Shapes

The arch, the ring and the line.

Three simple shapes, all taken from things already in the salon and the logo. Use one per layout, large and calm.

Materials

The salon already has its own palette of materials: matte black steel (the arch shelf), antique gold (the mirror frames), white joinery and glass. Signage should feel like part of that room: matte black panels, white or eucalyptus lettering, and brushed brass as the one metal if a metal is wanted. Avoid gloss, chrome and LED colour-changing effects.

Photography

Real people, real salon, natural window light. Hair is the subject: finished results from behind or three-quarter, hands at work, the arch shelf, the Wella range. Warm and slightly soft rather than sharp and glossy. No stock models, no heavy filters, and always ask the client before posting.

05 · Voice

Warm, sure, unhurried.

Hair Division sounds like Raine does in the chair: thirty years of experience, said plainly. Confident without selling, friendly without gushing.

Sounds like us
  • Come in, sit down, take your time.
  • Thirty years of good hair days.
  • Your exact quote is confirmed before we begin.
  • Expert hair care in a friendly, relaxed environment.
Doesn’t sound like us
  • Pamper yourself today!!
  • Auckland’s #1 hair experts
  • HUGE SALE: 50% off all colour
  • Luscious tresses await, queen

Plain words

Cut, colour, care. Say what it is. Sentence case, no exclamation marks.

Craft before price

Lead with experience and how the visit feels. Offers, when there are any, are stated once and quietly.

Local and personal

Takapuna, Lake Road, Raine, Ivy: use real names. People book with a person, not a brand.

06 · Shopfront

How it comes together on Lake Road.

A sketch of the full set working together, not a survey of the real building. Your signwriter will measure up and scale each piece; the proportions and materials are what matter.

1

Fascia sign

Matte black panel, lockup in white and eucalyptus. Flat-cut or raised letters; brushed brass is the upgrade.

2

Blade sign

Round, double-sided, at right angles to the street so people walking Lake Road see it before they arrive.

3

Window logo

The white roundel in matte white cut vinyl, centred at eye level. Keep the rest of the glass clear.

4

Door hours

White cut vinyl on the door glass: monogram, hours, phone and website. Nothing else on the door.

5

A-frame

One line and a phone number, readable in the three seconds someone takes to walk past.
